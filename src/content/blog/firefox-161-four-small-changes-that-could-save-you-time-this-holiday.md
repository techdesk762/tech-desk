If you plan to spend part of the holidays shopping online, reading long articles, or sitting in a parked car with nothing to do, a few of the new Firefox 161 features may help. None of them will change your life. All of them aim to save you a few taps.

Some are easy to switch on now. Others are still rolling out, so you may not see them yet. Here is a plain walkthrough.

## What this means for you

**Less fiddling with the VPN.** A VPN (virtual private network) hides your internet address and scrambles your traffic. Firefox's free built-in VPN on desktop now lets you set rules for individual sites. Pick the sites that should always use it and the sites that never should. Then you stop flipping it on and off by hand.

**Pages that read to you on Android.** Reader View is a stripped-down, clutter-free version of an article. It can now read pages aloud on Firefox for Android. That helps if you want to listen while cooking, wrapping gifts, or walking.

**A faster way to wipe your tracks.** A Fire button is coming to regular (Classic) windows on desktop. It clears your history and other stored data in one move. That is handy on a shared family computer.

**Firefox on a car dashboard.** If your car has Google built-in, you can download Firefox from Google Play. It is meant for use when the car is parked.

## What changed

Mozilla [highlighted four updates in Firefox 161](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/firefox/firefox-161-updates/) in a post dated November 24, 2026.

- **VPN rules:** Desktop users can choose "Always on" or "Always off" per site.
- **Read aloud:** Reader View's listening feature, already on desktop, is rolling out on Android.
- **Fire button:** It was previously in Private Window. It is now gradually arriving in Classic Window on desktop, with a different function.
- **Cars:** Firefox is available from Google Play on cars with Google built-in.

## What to do today

1. **Update Firefox** on your computer and phone, so the new features can reach you. Some are rolling out gradually, so don't worry if one is missing at first.
2. **Set up VPN rules on desktop.** Open the VPN panel on the toolbar, or go to Settings → Privacy & Security → VPN → Manage VPN rules. Add a website and choose "Always on" or "Always off." Firefox remembers your choice on every future visit.
3. **Sign in with a Mozilla account.** The VPN needs one. Availability depends on your region, so you may not see the VPN at all.
4. **Try read aloud on Android.** Open an article in Reader View from the address bar. In the pop-out menu, tap the headphones icon. You can play, pause, rewind, fast-forward, change speed and pick a voice. Audio can keep going with the screen off.
5. **Look for the Fire button on desktop.** If it has reached you, check what each option clears before you press anything. The notes don't say where the button sits, so see Mozilla's post or the release notes.

## Our take

The per-site VPN rules are the most practical change. A VPN you have to remember to toggle is a VPN you will forget about. Rules let you set it once. In our view, the sensible approach is to switch it "Always on" for sites where you enter payment details or log in on public Wi-Fi, and leave it "Always off" for sites that tend to break or slow down with a VPN. That second part is our suggestion, not Mozilla's.

The read-aloud feature has a nice privacy detail. It uses only voices that run on your device, so the page text and audio stay local. Voices that run on the device can sound less polished than online ones. We haven't tested that, so treat it as a possibility, not a verdict.

The Fire button is useful but needs care. Deleting everything signs you out of sites, closes your current window and opens a fresh one. Clearing saved form info and site permissions can also mean retyping details later. In the busiest shopping weeks, that could be an annoyance. Our recommendation: use the option to pick specific data instead of deleting everything. That keeps your current window open. The car feature is a niche addition. It will matter only to people with the right vehicle.

## What we don't know yet

- **The VPN's future.** Data is unlimited through January 31, 2027. The post doesn't say what happens after that date.
- **Which regions get the VPN.** Mozilla says availability depends on your region but gives no list.
- **When features reach you.** Read aloud on Android and the Fire button are rolling out gradually. The post gives no timetable.
- **Which cars work.** Mozilla names cars with Google built-in, but we have no list of makes or models.
- **The car privacy promise.** Mozilla says browsing history won't be shared with the car manufacturer. It also says not even Mozilla should know what sites you visit. We can't verify how that works.
- **Everything else in 161.** More changes are in the release notes. Mozilla invites feedback on Mozilla Connect.
