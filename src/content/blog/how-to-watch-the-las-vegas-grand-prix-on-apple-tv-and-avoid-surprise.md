If you want to watch the Las Vegas Grand Prix this weekend, you have a few ways to do it, and one detail about cost needs a careful look first. Apple's announcement says two things that don't fully line up. Below, we explain what it says, what to check, and how to avoid paying for something you didn't mean to buy.

## What this means for you

The race is streaming live on Apple TV in the U.S. Every session is available live and on demand, according to [Apple's announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/formula-1-las-vegas-grand-prix-streams-live-on-apple-tv-this-weekend/).

Here is the confusing part. The announcement says the race is free for U.S. fans. It also says watching requires a U.S. Apple TV subscription at $14.99 a month, with a seven-day free trial. Buyers of eligible new Apple devices get three months free.

The notes we have don't explain how those fit together. Maybe some content is free and the rest is not. Maybe the wording is just loose. We can't tell. Check the official page before you enter payment details.

If you do sign up for the trial, the practical risk is simple. A trial that you forget about can turn into a paid month. Cancelling in time is up to you.

There are also free options. Univision will simulcast the Apple TV broadcast for Spanish-speaking viewers, starting at 10 p.m. ET. Tubi is offering a free, creator-led alternative show called The Fast Lane. It is hosted by Michelle Khare and Justin Bell, and it is the third and last such show of 2026. An "altcast" is a different, more casual take on the same race.

## What changed

Apple is promoting its F1 coverage for the Las Vegas race, the only U.S. night race and the last U.S. race of the season. Three races remain after it.

Coverage offers English and Spanish commentary and up to 30 extra live feeds. These include Driver Tracker, timing data, and a mixed onboard camera. A Podium feed follows whoever is in first, second, and third. It exists only for race and Sprint sessions.

Multiview shows up to four feeds on one screen. It now works on certain smart TVs, not just Apple devices and streaming players. Dolby Atmos (surround sound) and Dolby Vision (richer picture) are supported on compatible gear.

Apple also announced extras across its services, such as Apple Maps guides for attendees and Apple Music playlists.

## What to do today

1. **Read the source page first.** Open [Apple's announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/formula-1-las-vegas-grand-prix-streams-live-on-apple-tv-this-weekend/) and look for the latest word on cost. Do this before you sign up for anything.
2. **Decide if a free option is enough.** If you prefer Spanish commentary, Univision's simulcast starts at 10 p.m. ET. If you like a relaxed, creator-style show, Tubi's The Fast Lane is free.
3. **If you start the Apple TV trial, set a reminder now.** The trial lasts seven days. A calendar alert a day or two before it ends gives you time to cancel. Our notes don't say where the cancel option lives, so use Apple's own subscription help or the source page for the steps.
4. **Check for a three-month offer.** If you recently bought, or plan to buy, a new Apple device, see whether you are eligible for the three months free. The notes only say "eligible," so confirm the details with Apple.
5. **Get the free Apple Sports app.** It shows live leaderboards, standings, and session updates. Live Activities, which put updates on your lock screen, need iOS 18 or later and watchOS 11 or later.
6. **Check your TV if you want Multiview.** Only "certain" smart TVs are supported. The notes don't list which ones, so look at Apple's page or your TV maker's information.

## Our take

What matters most is the cost wording. "Free" and "$14.99 a month with a trial" are very different things for a household. In our view, anyone not already subscribed should assume a payment method is needed until Apple's page says otherwise. Free trials are fine, but only if you remember to end them.

The extras are nice, though easy to overrate. Up to 30 live feeds and four-screen Multiview will appeal to dedicated fans. Most casual viewers will probably watch the main broadcast and ignore the rest. We'd expect the Podium feed and the mixed onboard camera to be the easiest ones to enjoy without learning a new setup.

Our recommendation: if you only want to see the race, try a free route first. Univision's simulcast or the Tubi show could be enough. If you want the full Apple experience, start the trial, set a cancel reminder in the same minute, and check the confirmation screen for the date it ends.
