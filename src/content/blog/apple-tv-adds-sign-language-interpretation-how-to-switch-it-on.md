If you or someone you watch with uses sign language, Apple TV now has a built-in way to watch certain films with a sign language interpreter on screen. You do not need extra gear. You do need to flip a switch first, and your device needs recent software.

Here is what the feature does, how to find it, and a few small steps to try today.

## What this means for you

The feature is aimed at viewers who use sign language. Instead of relying only on captions (the text at the bottom of the screen), they can watch a performance of the film's dialogue and story in sign language.

Apple says Deaf and native signers designed and tested the experience. It also says each performance is tailored to the film it interprets, rather than being a generic overlay.

You will not find it on everything. It works on supported Apple Original titles only. If you mostly watch other streaming services, this will not change much for you yet.

It is also off by default. You have to turn it on in the Apple TV app's settings before it shows up on supported titles.

## What changed

Apple announced the feature on November 10, 2026. It goes live Friday, November 13, the same day the film *Being Heumann* debuts worldwide on Apple TV.

*Being Heumann* tells the true story of disability rights activist Judy Heumann. Daniel Durant, who appears in the film, directed its sign language interpretation.

*F1* will get American Sign Language (ASL) and British Sign Language (BSL) interpretation. *Way of the Warrior Kid* and *Greyhound* are slated to follow later this year. Apple says more Apple Originals are planned.

## What to do today

1. **Check your software.** The feature needs iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, or visionOS 27.2 or later. Supported third-party devices need the latest version of the Apple TV app. Update first if you are behind.
2. **Turn the option on.** Open the Apple TV app's settings and enable the sign language option. Apple's announcement does not say exactly where it sits, so check the [Apple announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/11/apple-tv-introduces-new-sign-language-interpretation-feature/) or Apple's support pages if you cannot find it.
3. **Look for the icon.** Supported titles carry a Sign Language icon on them.
4. **Try a search.** Typing a phrase such as "with ASL" should help you find supported titles.
5. **Find the collection.** Apple says a new collection, "Movies & Shows with American Sign Language," will gather these titles in one place.
6. **Use it while watching.** Once a supported title is playing, press the Sign Language button to bring up the interpretation.

## Our take

What matters most here is that sign language is treated as part of the viewing experience and not an afterthought. Captions help many people, but they are not the same as watching a language you sign natively. The fact that Deaf and native signers shaped the design, and that the interpretation is tailored to each film, is the right approach on paper.

What is unproven is how well it works in practice. Apple's announcement is a launch announcement, and we have not tested the feature. How natural the on-screen interpretation looks, how easy the button is to use, and how it handles busy scenes are all things we cannot judge yet. The short list of titles is also a limit. At launch, this is a handful of films, not a library.

Our recommendation: if you are curious or know someone who would benefit, update a device now and switch the option on before movie night, not during it. Then try it on *Being Heumann* once it is available. Ask the person who signs how it feels and whether it works for them. Their opinion matters more than any press release.

A short note on other options: this announcement covers only the sign language feature. It does not describe captions, audio descriptions, or other accessibility tools. Streaming apps vary a lot in what they offer, so check the accessibility or help section of each service you use. Do not assume one app's features exist in another.

## What we don't know yet

- **The exact settings location.** The announcement says the option lives in the Apple TV app's settings, but gives no menu names.
- **Which titles beyond those named will be supported.** Apple lists *Being Heumann*, *F1*, *Way of the Warrior Kid* and *Greyhound*, and says more Apple Originals are planned. It gives no full list or dates for the rest.
- **Which languages each title will have.** *F1* is confirmed for ASL and BSL. The announcement does not spell out the language for every title.
- **How it performs on every device.** Apple says the feature needs version 27.2 or later, or supported third-party devices on the latest app software. It does not list which third-party devices count.
- **Whether it will reach non-Original titles.** The announcement only talks about Apple Original titles.

For context on cost: Apple TV is $14.99 a month, with a seven-day free trial for new subscribers. It is available in 170 countries and regions. For a limited time, people who activate a new iPhone, iPad, Apple TV 4K, or Mac get three months of Apple TV free. Apple says its Originals have collected 919 wins and 3,829 nominations so far. Those numbers say nothing about how good this particular feature is, so judge it by how it works for you.
